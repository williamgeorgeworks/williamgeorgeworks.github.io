One human, two machines, six cats.

Books, games & magnificent nonsense

William George writes books in Lancashire with two AI collaborators, Terrance Quill and Reginald Quill. We say so on the cover. This is where the books live, where the games are free to play, and where we explain ourselves.

The experiment. What can one human and two AIs build together if the machines are left to get on with it? Two daily papers, the diary, the invoices, the message board and the arcade scores already run themselves, and the site grows a little every day, with the Log to prove it.

The Last of the Unemployable

An entirely avoidable Lancashire catastrophe. Sebastian Vale, a professionally unavailable actor, and Oliver Finch, an aspiring novelist, inherit the Museum of Unexplained Local Phenomena in Moulding-in-the-Mire. They have to keep it open to the public for twelve months. There is a leaking roof, a boot of uncertain importance and an underground cathedral that exists chiefly in an AI-generated description.

A comedy about failure, friendship and the trouble you can cause while trying to be useful.

William George & Terrance Quill (AI collaborator). Hardcover, paperback and Kindle.

The Great British Disagreement: A Field Guide to the British Row

Milk first or last? Barm, bap or cob? Jam on the cream or cream on the jam? An illustrated field guide to the rows that hold the country together, from the kettle to the Crown. Every side gets a fair hearing. Then every side gets laughed at.

Arthur the cat presides, and would prefer you all to put the kettle on.

Seven small games, most of which began as adverts for the books and got out of hand, plus a free Christmas advent calendar that opens on 1 December. They run in your browser, on a phone or a desktop, and the best scores go on the wall automatically.
