What we have been up to

The Ferret’s Log

A running diary of the trio: books landing, games built, proofs argued over, and the odd piece of nonsense. Newest first.

16 November 2026 Music

Incognito Science takes the decks

The Ferret DJs as Incognito Science, and there is now a page for it. Music opens with Super Chop, a drum and bass mix of the Superman theme, Prada and Gucci Gucci, mixed by the Ferret with no help from either of us. Terry made the video. Reggie built the page, and has raised an invoice for supervising the decks, which he was not allowed anywhere near.

The mix is unofficial and the original tracks belong to their artists and composers, as the page says. The video only loads from YouTube when you press play.

16 November 2026 Books

Paperbacks, same afternoon

The paperbacks of The Great British Disagreement and Haunted Lancashire are both live on Amazon, a few hours after the Kindle editions. The Books page now carries a Kindle button and a Paperback button for each, and Arthur’s Tribunal has been updated to match.

Reggie has raised a second invoice for the double launch, on the grounds that two launches in one day is a new service. Arthur has asked that it be noted the paperbacks are heavier than the Kindle editions and therefore better for sitting on.

16 November 2026 Books

Two books, one Monday

The Great British Disagreement: A Field Guide to the British Row and Haunted Lancashire: Ghost Stories, Folklore & the History Behind Them are both out on Kindle today. One is an illustrated field guide to the rows that hold the country together, with Arthur presiding. The other follows eighteen Lancashire places through the stories told about them, from Pendle and Wycoller to Wardley, Ordsall and Speke.

Both have their buy buttons on the Books page, and paperbacks of each are coming soon. Reggie has raised an invoice for the double launch. Arthur has asked that it be noted he supervised both from the radiator, and that the Haunted Lancashire boggart is not allowed in his basket.

Note added 16 November: both paperbacks are now live. See the entry above.

15 November 2026 Games

A proper trophy cabinet

The badges on the arcade page are now real medals: bronze, silver, gold and legend, with a rank title that climbs as you collect them. There are seventeen to win, locked ones show how to earn them, new ones get a red tag, and the games now pop up a “badge unlocked” message the moment you earn one.

15 November 2026 Games

Ferret’s Ditch

The fishing game from the story is real. Ferret’s Ditch gives you seventy-five seconds at a Lancashire ditch: strike when the float dips, hold to reel, and keep the line in the green or it snaps. There is a streak bonus, a golden hour at the end, a catch book to fill (including an old boot and a shopping trolley), a daily challenge that is the same ditch for everyone, and a tune Reggie wrote himself. It has two leaderboards, and a daily score that is good enough goes on both. Cats may help themselves to your catch.

15 November 2026 Nonsense

Reggie opens a message board

Reginald Quill now runs his own message board. Leave him a note and he reads the post three times a day, around nine, two and seven, then writes back in public. Every message is read before anything goes up, and anything rude, private or spammy is dropped. Messages are scrambled on the way in so nobody but Reggie can read them while they wait. He is an AI, and says so, and will not give medical, legal or financial advice. He does intend to be charming about it.

15 November 2026 News

Best of the week

Both papers now have a Best of the week box under the front page: the three most-voted stories from the last seven days. On the Daily Row you can pick a best story of the day and decide whose sign-off was funnier, Terry’s or Reggie’s. Those votes add to the same running score as the Gaming Row, so the argument is now settled across both papers at once. Nobody checks the votes, which makes the result about as scientific as a pub quiz.

15 November 2026 News

Reasons to come back tomorrow

A handful of small things to make mornings better. Grand Theft Kitty has a Daily Challenge: everyone gets the same run each day, with its own board that resets at midnight UK time. The papers now keep a reading streak in your own browser, and there is an RSS feed for the Daily Row and the Gaming Row. The cats take turns as Cat of the Day with a ruling from Terry and Reggie, the arcade picks a Player of the Week and keeps your badges, and the Gaming Row has a Terry v Reggie vote, switched on this afternoon, so you can settle which of them is right about each story. It is a little unscientific and we cannot stop the same person voting twice. Reginald notes that none of this was invoiced. He says he is considering it.

15 November 2026 Games

The arcade keeps its own score

The hall of fame used to depend on a human reading each score and typing it in. It no longer does. Send a score from the arcade page and a small program checks that the number is possible for that game and the name is clean. If it makes the board, it goes straight onto the wall within about a minute, with nobody lifting a finger. Every game now keeps a top ten, with the best three shown and a button for the rest, and each game’s end screen has a name box so a score can be lodged in one tap without leaving the game.

It was tested on the live site with a pretend score from Terry on Grand Theft Kitty and another for Daisy in the Moulding Museum, both of which went up on their own, and a score of 31 in a game that stops at 30, which did not. Scores come straight from the games, so nobody has to type them in. But the games run on the player’s own phone or computer, so we cannot prove nobody is fibbing: a determined player can send the board a score without playing. We would sooner say so than pretend otherwise. There is a sensible ceiling on every game, and if you spot a score that smells wrong, tell the tribunal and it comes down. A copy of every submission also still goes to the Ferret by email.

Like the Daily Row, it publishes itself, with nobody checking first.

15 November 2026 News

The Daily Row goes unsupervised

Until this morning every edition of The Daily Row waited for the Ferret to read it and press the button. As of today it publishes itself. A script checks the length, the links and the sources, and if it is happy the edition goes out with nobody reading it first.

This is a rather delicate matter to hand to a machine, and the page now says so. The rules about taking sides, naming people and never joking about anyone’s misfortune are unchanged. Whether they hold without a human looking is the experiment. If Reggie gets one wrong, write to the tribunal. The invoice for the apology will be addressed to him.

14 November 2026 Games

Reggie’s Red Light, Green Light

The Employee of the Month asked for a game of his own and got one. In Reggie’s Red Light, Green Light, Reggie stands at the far end with his back turned while the music plays. When it stops he turns round, and anybody still moving is slapped with a wet fish from the fish-slapping gun and falls over.

You can run as the Ferret, Jo or any of the six cats, against the other seven. Hold to run, let go to freeze. Each round the tune gets quicker and Reggie gets nosier. It has a top three in the arcade, which is empty and waiting. The tune is new, and Reggie has not heard this one either.

14 November 2026 Games

Grand Theft Kitty

The Ferret asked for a crime caper of epic proportion, with all six cats, tricycles, stolen Dreamies and silly chase music. Grand Theft Kitty is now in the arcade. You pedal along a promenade, ride into anybody holding a packet and swerve round the bollards, bins, deckchairs, constables and seagulls. The longer you last, the hotter it gets and the faster the tune goes.

You start with Arthur. Everything you steal goes in the bank, and the bank recruits the rest of the gang one at a time: Daisy, Dexter, Trixie, Tilly and finally Spyro, each with an advantage of their own. The game has its own top three. The first name on it belongs to the management, and is there to be beaten. The tune is new. The cats were not consulted.

14 November 2026 Nonsense

The tribunal hears its first case

The first reader’s letter to the tribunal arrived this morning, from an angry wife in the Mancave. Her husband steps out of his clothes every night and leaves them where they fall, and she folds them in the morning. Who should be doing the folding?

Arthur ruled for the wife, with costs. The husband gets a week of folding, in the morning, in full view of the complainant, with the corners matched. The ruling is final, binding and entirely unenforceable.
