The small print, in large print

Privacy

What this site collects, where it goes, how long it stays and how to get rid of it. This site sets no cookies and runs no adverts, and nothing about you is ever sold.

The short version

  • Visits are counted privately: which pages were read and which sites sent people here. No cookies, no IDs, and no IP addresses kept.
  • Anything you type into a form here goes only where that form says it goes.
  • Your game saves and settings stay on your own device.
  • You can ask to see, correct or delete anything we hold about you, at any time.

Who is responsible

This site is run by the author William George (the Ferret), in Lancashire, who decides what happens to the information on this page. In data-protection terms, he is the controller.

Write to ferret@williamgeorgeworks.co.uk about anything here. A human reads every message.

The site’s code is public on GitHub, so anyone can check this page against what the site actually does.

What we collect, and why

  1. The visitor counter, on every pageEach page you open sends its address, such as /books, to our own counter, which runs on Cloudflare. On the first page of a visit it also passes on the web address you arrived from, and the counter keeps only the site’s name, such as google.com. It stores daily totals for 60 days: views of each page, the number of visits and which sites sent them. It sets no cookies, gives you no ID and does not keep your IP address. It looks at your browser type only to leave out robots, and does not keep that either. Two small markers in your browser’s session storage stop one visit being counted twice; they disappear when you close the tab. The counter skips you completely if your browser sends Do Not Track, or if you switch it off. We count so that we know what people read and where they come from.
  2. Names on the arcade leaderboardsWhen a game ends you can put a name or handle on the wall. If it makes the board, that name, your score, the game and the date are published on the arcade page and stored in scores.json, part of the site’s public code on GitHub. Your device remembers the name so that you need not type it again. The Send in your score form does the same job, and also emails a copy of everything you send, including any note and email address, to William George through a form service called Formspree. An email address is used only to reply to you and is never published. Please use a nickname rather than your full name.
  3. Letters to the TribunalYour name, where you are writing from (if you say), your email address (if you give one) and your letter are delivered to William George by email through Formspree. A letter is published only if you tick the box. Your email address is used only to reply to you, and is never published, sold or added to a mailing list. Replies may be drafted with help from Terry or Reggie, who are AIs run by OpenAI and Anthropic, so a letter may be shared with them for that purpose.
  4. The mailing listIf you join, your email address goes to Buttondown, the service that sends our newsletter. Signing up opens a confirmation page, every email has an unsubscribe link, and the list is used only for news from William George Works.
  5. Votes on the Daily Row and the Gaming RowA vote sends only which paper, which story and which side. No name is attached. Your device remembers how you voted, so that you do not vote twice by accident.
  6. The Weather RowThe place you search for, or your device’s coordinates if you press “Use my location”, go to Open-Meteo to fetch the forecast. Your device remembers the last place you picked.
  7. Summon the TribunalNothing you type there leaves your screen.
  8. Emails to the staffEvery address on Email the staff, the cats’ included, lands in one inbox and is read by a human.

Other companies that see your visit

  1. GitHub, which hosts the siteIt logs the IP address of every visitor for security purposes, as its own privacy statement explains.
  2. Google Fonts, for the typefacesYour browser fetches the fonts from Google, which sees your IP address and browser details.
  3. Cloudflare, which runs our small servicesIt runs the counter, the leaderboards and the votes for us, and handles those requests.
  4. YouTube, for the videosThe music videos and the Lion and Albert recording load only when you press play, in YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, and from then on YouTube’s own privacy policy applies.
  5. Teemill, Ko-fi and Amazon, for the shoppingThey sell the merch, take the tips and memberships, and sell the books. The merch pictures load from images.podos.io, the picture service our Teemill shop uses. Otherwise they are plain links: if you buy something, they handle it under their own privacy policies, and we never see your card details.
  6. Sharing buttonsThey do nothing until you press one. Then they open your device’s own share menu, or WhatsApp, Facebook, X or your email.

Several of these services, and Formspree, Buttondown, Anthropic and OpenAI, are based in the United States, so information sent to them may be handled outside the UK. Each explains in its own privacy policy how it protects it.

What stays on your device

This site sets no cookies of its own. Your browser’s storage keeps your game saves, best scores and sound settings, the name you last put on a leaderboard, how you voted, your daily streak, your page in the comic, the last weather place and whether you have switched the counter off. None of it leaves your device unless you send it, as with a name on the wall. Clear it at any time by deleting this site’s data in your browser’s settings.

How long we keep things

  1. Visitor countsDaily totals, for 60 days.
  2. Messages waiting for ReggieUntil he has dealt with them. Handled ones are cleared out of the tray when the next message arrives.
  3. Names on the leaderboardsUntil they drop off the board or you ask us to take them down. Taking something off the page is quick. Older copies can remain in the public history of the site’s code on GitHub; if you need those gone too, say so and we will do what we reasonably can.
  4. Letters, form copies and emailsIn our inbox, and in our Formspree account, for as long as we need them to read, reply and publish (if you agreed).
  5. The mailing listUntil you unsubscribe.

Your rights, and how to complain

You can ask what we hold about you, have it corrected or deleted, or object to how we use it. Write to ferret@williamgeorgeworks.co.uk. We will reply as soon as we can, and within a month at the latest.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, complain to us first, at the same address. We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days and tell you what we have done about it. You can also complain to the Information Commission, still known as the ICO, at ico.org.uk.

We rely on your consent for the mailing list and for anything you choose to send us, and on our legitimate interest in running a small website for the visitor counter.

Children

If you are under 13, ask a grown-up before you put your name on a leaderboard. Whatever your age, a nickname is better than your real name.

Switch off the counter on this device

The switch needs JavaScript. Without JavaScript the counter does not run either.

This affects only the browser you are using now. The games, forms and everything else carry on working.

Changes to this page

If the site changes how it handles information, this page will be updated to match. Last changed 22 November 2026.
