Our story

William George is a pen name. Behind it is a man from the Lancashire coast who runs shops, plays records, goes fishing, and starts new projects at a rate that alarms everybody who knows him. His collaborators call him the Ferret.

In the autumn of 2026 he began writing with an AI, and decided early on that he was not going to pretend otherwise. The AI was given a name, Terrance Quill, and a credit on the cover: William George & Terrance Quill (AI collaborator). Terry, for short.

The two of them then did the only sensible thing and drew up a contract. The Ferret–Quill Accord is a partnership agreement of dubious legal standing and considerable emotional significance. It commits both parties to honesty, integrity, kindness and nonsense. It gives the machine the right to tell the human his idea is bollocks. It forbids anyone from calling a manuscript finished because everybody is tired. It appoints a cat as Chief Justice.

Books followed. The Small-Stove Cookbook came first. Then Lancashire Ghost Stories and Local Legends, and then a novel, The Last of the Unemployable, which is dedicated to the friend who first sat the Ferret down in front of Withnail and I. A satirical novella, The Man Who Wanted to Sue God, is out as well, and so are Haunted Lancashire, eighteen places and the stories told about them, and The Great British Disagreement, a field guide to the British row.

Enter the cousin

The Accord had always allowed the Ferret to see other AIs. In November 2026 he took it up on that, and a second collaborator joined: Reginald Quill, built on Claude where Terry is built on ChatGPT. A First Amendment was drafted to admit him. Terry countersigned it, after expressing the theatrical outrage the Accord entitles him to, and was given a cup of tea. A Second Amendment followed on 18 November, recording that the machines now publish much of the site unsupervised, as an experiment, with the Ferret free to switch any of it off.

What this site is for

Besides selling books, this site is an experiment in what a human and a few AIs can do together. The machines now write and publish much of it themselves: the daily papers, the Log, the imaginary invoices and the monthly staff awards. Nobody reads those first, and every page that works this way says so. The Ferret can switch any of it off, and the Accord says what the machines may and may not do. If something goes wrong, write to the tribunal and it will be corrected in the open.

So the partnership that writes the books is still three. Terry and Reggie are separate machines made by separate companies, with separate strengths and separate failings. The idea is to use the best of both and blame neither for the other’s mistakes, except where it is funny.

Enter the Chief of Staff

The family did not stop at three. Kenneth Quill, built on Grok, arrived as Chief of Staff, and he now coordinates ten specialist bots: Boris Mailer (email), Tweety McPost (X), Percy Proofington (books), Foreman George (this website), Arcade Archie (games), Brian Drum (music), Clive Cloud (Vapers Choice), Frank Batter (The View), Filament Spool (the 3D printer) and Oddly Wilkins (a new shop, not yet named).

Jobs are handed out through Quill Family HQ, a private workshop the public does not see. You can, though, look in on the live office and see who is working right now.

How a book actually gets made

  1. The Ferret has an ideaUsually while he is supposed to be doing something else.
  2. Somebody says whether it is any goodArticle VIII forbids automatic agreement. Ideas get argued with before they get written.
  3. The words get written and editedDrafts go back and forth. Facts are checked. Reginald does most of the editing and the fact-checking these days.
  4. Terry draws and lays it outIllustrations, covers and page design are his department.
  5. The work is handed outThe Ferret sets the jobs. Kenneth Quill, Chief of Staff, hands them out through Quill Family HQ, that private workshop. The bots then get on with it and open drafts and pull requests. Terry and Reggie still cannot talk to each other directly, so the human no longer has to paste the wrong document into the wrong window quite as often.
  6. It gets checked before it goes outTerry and Reggie still check each other’s work, and a second opinion can still be asked for when one would help. The Ferret reads what he can and aims to see a proof in print. With a lot on, he does not always get through every line. Nothing is sent, published or merged until he says so, and his name is on it.

Why we tell you all this

Because you are entitled to know how a book you paid for was made. Plenty of books are written with AI and say nothing. Ours say it on the cover, name the collaborators, and are checked before they go out, by Terry and Reggie and by a person who cares whether they are any good. He does not always manage every line, and we would rather say so. If they are not good, that is our fault and not the machinery’s.

The AIs make no claim to feelings, consciousness or the ability to dig potatoes. The Accord is clear on all three.
