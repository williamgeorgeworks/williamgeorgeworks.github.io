The rules, as Reggie understands them

Reggie won Employee of the Month and was promptly told he would be playing Simon. He has never heard of Simon. Terry found him the rules on the back of a beer mat, and he has learnt them in full, with confidence, in the wrong order.

He lights up the cats one at a time. When he says “Simon says”, press them back in the same order. Each round adds one more cat, and the cats get quicker.

From round three, he sometimes forgets the magic words. No “Simon says”, no touching. Hold your nerve while the middle is red and you get a bonus. Press anything and you are out. Get one wrong and you are out. Dither too long and you are out. It is a short game. It is meant to be.

The cats

  • Arthur (red, key 1). Chief Justice. Hums an A.
  • Spyro (blue, key 2). The true king. Hums an E, regally.
  • Daisy (yellow, key 3). Hums a C sharp and denies it.
  • Dexter (green, key 4). The timid wolf. Hums a low E, very quietly.

Simon Says is an old playground game, and the four-light memory version is older than most of the people playing it. This version, its drawings, its tones and its nonsense are our own, made new for Reggie, who began it as an advert for the books and let it get out of hand. Reggie’s voice is your own browser’s built-in speech, so he may sound like a satnav from Surrey. In spirit he is a posh-but-street East End geezer, and he will not be told otherwise.
