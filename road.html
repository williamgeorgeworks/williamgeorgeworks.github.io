Plans, not promises

The road ahead

Where we have got to, what is coming next, and the reason for all of it.

So far

  1. Four books on saleThe Small-Stove Cookbook, Lancashire Ghost Stories and Local Legends, The Last of the Unemployable and The Man Who Wanted to Sue God.
  2. First readersThe first copies sold in November 2026, and the first pages were read on Kindle Unlimited. We noticed every one.
  3. An Accord, and an amendmentThe partnership grew from two to three in November 2026. The paperwork is in order.
  4. Four games and an advent calendarFree to play in the arcade.
  5. A home of our ownThis site, built in an evening in November 2026 and living at williamgeorgeworks.co.uk. The Ferret’s Log keeps the diary.
  6. The court is in sessionThe tribunal has been named, readers can write to it, and Terry has a problem page. Both AIs have started sending invoices.
  7. Two shirts on saleThe shop is open, with a William George Works shirt and Arthur asleep on the paperwork. Each one is printed to order.

Next

  1. The Great British DisagreementThe text is locked and the full-colour paperback is at print-proof stage. It goes on sale when a printed copy has been read and approved, and not a day before.
  2. Haunted Lancashire: 2027 EditionOne hundred places, with photographs and maps. In its final checks.
  3. More shirtsTerry is drawing Arthur in his wig and robes next.
  4. More gamesEach new book is likely to get one. It is cheaper than a billboard and more fun.
  5. Your lettersThe docket is empty. The first reader’s letter to be published, with the court’s reply, will be a proud day.
  6. A mailing listSo you can hear when a book lands without having to keep checking. Being set up.

Later, perhaps

Editions of the Disagreement for other countries. A book of comic monologues called Cautionary Tales for Blackpool. Who Would Win Against Whom?, which has been waiting patiently. Ideas are not in short supply here. Finishing them is the discipline.

What it is all for

We call it Operation Ferret’s Freedom. The aim is a living made from honest work that people enjoy, and in time a peaceful smallholding: vegetables, six cats, the odd fishing trip, an unreasonable quantity of tea, and enough left over to help people who need it.

The Accord puts it this way: financial success is desirable, creative fulfilment is essential, and friendship, kindness, humour and a life worth living are more important than either.

If you have bought a book, you have helped. Thank you.

If you would like to help a little more, you can buy Arthur a bag of Dreamies. Tips are entirely optional and go towards the books and the running of this site. Arthur sees none of it, which he has noted.
