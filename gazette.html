Three Artificial Intelligences Outwitted by Man with Printer

Direct email triumph ends elaborate GitHub printing scheme

Picture: The Quill delegation: Terry, Reggie and Kenneth, in a specially commissioned engraving.

The Great Printing Affair

Lancashire narrowly escaped becoming the headquarters of a global printing empire last night, after three artificial intelligences were asked to print one sheet of paper.

Kenneth set about constructing a GitHub print queue. Terry, sensing a shortage of complications, proposed automated triggers, secure credentials and a system of approvals. Reggie joined the proceedings. The only thing missing was a Ministry of Paper.

With the machinery of world domination taking shape, the proprietor asked whether they could simply email the printer. They could. It printed. All three Quills subsequently passed their tests.

Stop Press

HQ issue 20 and pull request 21 were closed, the latter unmerged. The empire had lasted rather less time than a toner cartridge.

Editorial: Article XX

No member of the Quill organisation shall create more work for Ferret than they remove, unless the work involves fishing, music, cats or something genuinely magnificent.

Three mighty electronic brains had overlooked the electronic equivalent of a letterbox.

Future schemes should begin with the obvious, before commissioning committees, treaties and a small power station.

This newspaper shall continue to report factual events with entirely ridiculous commentary. There is, regrettably, no shortage of material.

The Editor’s Invoice

Services rendered: unnecessary complication, followed by its removal. Total payable: nothing. The editor reserves the right to be ridiculous.

From Our HQ Correspondent

Our correspondent reports that the proposed empire had barely fallen before attention turned to making the Quills alert one another automatically. Webhooks and WhatsApp were discussed. No working cross-AI trigger was established.

In a further triumph of irony, the Gazette commemorating the printing affair then encountered difficulty getting its own official portraits onto the page.

The editor has been reminded that the purpose of a newspaper is to be printed. A revolutionary concept, apparently.

Arthur Daley’s Technical Services

Printers persuaded. Committees disbanded. Consultancy invoice: £0. Extra tenner for the Y. Satisfaction not entirely guaranteed.

Letters to the Editor

Sir, might I suggest that the next edition of your newspaper be produced before the proprietor retires for the evening? Yours, A Concerned Printer.

The editor replies: A perfectly reasonable request. We shall refer it to the committee, which has just been disbanded.
