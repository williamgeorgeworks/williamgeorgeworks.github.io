The court will hear you now

Letters to the Tribunal

Read one of the books? Beaten a game? Got a grievance, a compliment, or a row you would like settled? Write to the tribunal. A human reads every letter (for a quicker chat, Reggie runs a message board), and the best are published here with the court’s reply.

Three things share the name. Summon the Tribunal: get a comic verdict on a row of your own. Arthur’s Tribunal: play the game. Letters to the Tribunal: write to us, and the best are answered in public. You are here.

Rules of the court

  1. Nothing appears automaticallyEvery letter is read by a human first. Only letters whose writers ticked the box are ever published.
  2. The court may replySometimes in the voice of William George, sometimes in the voice of Arthur. Arthur’s replies are shorter.
  3. Letters may be trimmedFor length, never for meaning.
  4. Be decentHonesty, integrity, kindness and nonsense apply to correspondents too. Abuse goes in the bin.

What happens to your details

Your letter is delivered to William George by email through a form service called Formspree. Your email address, if you give one, is used only to reply to you. It is never published, sold or added to a mailing list. Ask at any time and your letter will be taken down or deleted. The privacy page has the rest.

The case of the wandering trousers

Dear Tribunal,

My husband insists on stepping out of his clothes each night and leaving them where they fall for me to then pick them up and fold them the following morning. Who should be folding the clothes? Me or him?

An angry wife, The Mancave
The court rules

Him. Comfortably, and with costs.

The court has reviewed the evidence, which is a man stepping out of his clothes like a snake shedding a skin and leaving the skin where it landed. This is the one thing a cat does not do. A cat who knocks a glass off a table at least has the decency to look you in the eye while doing it.

The husband creates the pile. The wife merely collects it. Under the ancient principle of you dropped it, you pick it up, the folding falls to him. The court also notes the address given. A mancave is a room the husband has chosen to call his own, so the clothes are in his jurisdiction, and the court has no sympathy for a man who cannot keep order in his own kingdom.

Sentence: one week of folding, in the morning, in full view of the complainant, with corners matched. Failure to comply may result in the clothes being relocated to the cat’s bed. The court sleeps on them and has no plans to move.

This ruling is final, binding and entirely unenforceable. Signed, Arthur.
