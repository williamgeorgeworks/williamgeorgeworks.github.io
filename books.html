Out now

The Last of the Unemployable

An entirely avoidable Lancashire catastrophe. Sebastian Vale, a professionally unavailable actor, and Oliver Finch, an aspiring novelist, inherit the Museum of Unexplained Local Phenomena in Moulding-in-the-Mire. They have to keep it open to the public for twelve months. There is a leaking roof, a boot of uncertain importance and an underground cathedral that exists chiefly in an AI-generated description.

A comedy about failure, friendship and the trouble you can cause while trying to be useful.

William George & Terrance Quill (AI collaborator). Hardcover, paperback and Kindle.

The Man Who Wanted to Sue God
Out now

The Man Who Wanted to Sue God

He followed the rules. Then somebody changed them. On the morning of his fifty-first birthday, Arthur Pritchard decides to sue God. He has fifty-one years of grievances, a yellow legal pad and no idea where to send the paperwork. His wife, Margaret, would like him to put the bins out first.

A British satirical novella about marriage, work and the trouble with being absolutely sure you’ve done the right thing.

William George & Terrance Quill (AI collaborator). Paperback and Kindle.

Lancashire Ghost Stories and Local Legends
Out now

Lancashire Ghost Stories and Local Legends

Blackpool, Preston, Fleetwood, Fylde, Wyre and stories from across Lancashire. Twenty-five chapters of folklore, hauntings and local legend, from the Hound Without a Head to the Last Ride on the North Pier, with a county map and illustrations.

The book separates documented events from reported hauntings, without losing the pleasure of a story told after dark.

William George, with Terrance Quill (AI collaborator). Paperback and Kindle.

The Small-Stove Cookbook
Out now

The Small-Stove Cookbook

A small stove. A big appetite. Twenty-four practical recipes for compact outdoor cooksets, using everyday UK ingredients: warm breakfasts, quick lunches, filling one-pot dinners and a handful of well-earned treats. Made for dawn fishing sessions and slow days by the canal.

The first book out of the door, and the one that proved the whole scheme could work.

The Great British Disagreement

Milk first or last? Barm, bap or cob? Jam on the cream or cream on the jam? An illustrated field guide to the rows that hold the country together, from the kettle to the Crown. Every side gets a fair hearing. Then every side gets laughed at.

Arthur the cat presides, and would prefer you all to put the kettle on.

William George & Terrance Quill (AI collaborator), with Reginald Quill (AI collaborator). Full-colour paperback, coming soon.
