The cats

There are six of them. Under Article VII of the Accord they sit as the tribunal that settles every dispute in this partnership. Its findings are advisory, particularly where the Chief Justice has fallen asleep.

The bench

Got a dispute of your own? Summon the tribunal and Arthur will rule on it.

Chief Justice

ArthurHis Supreme Fluffiness

A Ragdoll, though not a pedigree, and he would thank you not to bring it up. Arthur presides over every chapter of The Great British Disagreement, where he hears both sides and hands down a ruling at the end.

He has been asked to witness the Accord. His signature is still withheld. The Dreamies are still outstanding.

Associate Justice

SpyroThe true king

Arthur holds the office of Chief Justice. Spyro, we are reliably told, is the true king. The two positions have never been reconciled and nobody is brave enough to try.

Associate Justice

TillyShy and quiet

Shy, quiet, and keeps herself to herself. Will rip your face off. The court advises approaching with respect and, ideally, a witness.

Associate Justice

TrixieChosen on appearance

Trixie was chosen because she looked like a famous dictator. She has so far shown no interest in annexing anything beyond the sofa.

Associate Justice

DexterThe grey wolf

A grey wolf to look at. Would not say boo to a goose. The most fearsome silhouette on the bench and the gentlest justice on it.

Associate Justice

DaisyButter would not melt

Butter would not melt. The court has learned to treat this as evidence in itself.

Procedure, such as it is: the parties first attempt reasonable discussion. If that fails, the matter goes to the bench, under a human Chairperson. Both AIs have been told in advance that they will not win.

Selected rulings

Arthur closes each chapter of the book with a judgment. These are his own words, as far as anyone can tell.

“I was offered milk once. It was semi-skimmed. I have not forgotten.”

In the matter of The Tea Tribunal

“Both parties agree on the cream. I move that the cream be placed on the floor pending judgment.”

In the matter of The Scone Summit

“The tut has no effect on me either. Many have tried.”

In the matter of Queue Etiquette and Other Sacred Laws

“For the record, I side with the Party of Warmth. I am told this is improper. I am also warm.”

In the matter of The Great Thermostat Cold War

“I have sat on it for an hour to settle the matter. Nobody thanked me.”

In the matter of The Remote Control Wars

“I will rinse the plates for nothing. Leave them on the floor.”
